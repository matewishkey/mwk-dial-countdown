Tap the screen to pause or resume, tap it twice to reset it to full, hold it for the next preset. If the clock is running, paused, finished or dialled off its preset, that hold puts it right first; the next one moves on. Nothing here starts the clock but the tap.

Turn to adjust the clock — never the presets below, which only change here. Once the clock is off its preset the label reads from 20m, and holding the screen puts it back.

Turn for seconds, push the dial in and turn for minutes. That is the whole step model — there is no mode to set and nothing left switched on when you let go. Anything longer than a few minutes is a preset, typed above, and the dial is for nudging what a preset loaded.

Press the dial to start or pause. A press that did not turn the dial is the start/stop control, so the job you do most often is under the hand already on the dial. A press that did turn it was a minute-step adjustment, and letting go ends the turn, nothing more.

Press to pause or resume, press twice to reset it to full, hold to put the clock right, or load the next preset when there is nothing to put right. Neither starts it. Every gesture is acknowledged on the key itself — the ring pulses and the line under the clock names what just happened.
